About Me

I am Jerry Joel, a graduate in Applied Statistics from King Fahd University of Petroleum and Minerals (KFUPM), Dhahran, Saudi Arabia. My thesis focused on the Sine–Fréchet G Family of Continuous Distributions: Sub-models, Properties, Simulation and Applications, since published in the Journal of Statistical Theory and Applications (2026).

I completed my Bachelor of Technology in Statistics from Modibbo Adama University of Technology, Yola, Nigeria, graduating with First Class Honours (GPA: 4.73/5.00) as the best graduating student in Statistics for the 2018/2019 session. I am broadly interested in distribution theory, survival analysis, and simulation-based methods in applied statistics.

My current research combines classical distribution theory with simulation and real-data validation, with published work on generalized and transmuted probability distributions applied to survival data and public health data, including COVID-19 case data.

Research

MSc Thesis

The Sine–Fréchet G Family of Continuous Distributions: Sub-models, Properties, Simulation and Applications
My thesis introduced a new generalized family of continuous probability distributions built on the Fréchet distribution. The work:

  • Derives key statistical properties (moments, hazard function, quantile function) of the new family and its sub-models;
  • Estimates parameters via maximum likelihood and validates estimator performance through Monte Carlo simulation;
  • Demonstrates practical applications by fitting the distribution to real datasets and comparing performance against existing models.

Methodological and Applied Interests

  • Constructing and characterizing new families of continuous probability distributions (generalized, transmuted, compounded).
  • Applying flexible lifetime distributions to survival and time-to-event data.
  • Modeling public health and epidemiological data (e.g., COVID-19 case data) using generalized distributions.
  • Simulation-based evaluation of parameter estimation methods (maximum likelihood and beyond).

Additional Research Themes

    Beyond the areas listed above, I remain open to investigating related methodological or applied research themes within statistical distribution theory, reliability, and biostatistics.
