About Me

I am Jerry Joel, a graduate in Applied Statistics from King Fahd University of Petroleum and Minerals (KFUPM), Dhahran, Saudi Arabia. My master's thesis was situated broadly within Probability Distributions Theory, centred on the construction, characterization, and application of new families of continuous probability distributions. One outcome of this work, The Sine–Fréchet G Family of Continuous Distributions: Sub-models, Properties, Simulation and Applications, has since been published in the Journal of Statistical Theory and Applications (2026).

I completed my Bachelor of Technology in Statistics from Modibbo Adama University of Technology, Yola, Nigeria, graduating with First Class Honours (GPA: 4.73/5.00) as the best graduating student in Statistics for the 2018/2019 session. I am broadly interested in probability distributions theory, survival analysis, and simulation-based methods in applied statistics.

My research combines classical distribution theory with simulation and real-data validation, with published work on several generalized and transmuted probability distributions; including extensions of the Fréchet, Lomax, Ishita, Gompertz, and Lindley-exponential families; applied to survival data and public health data, including COVID-19 case data.

Research

MSc Thesis

Probability Distribution Theory
I completed my MSc research at King Fahd University of Petroleum and Minerals (KFUPM) under the supervision of Dr. Emmanuel Afuecheta. My thesis focused on constructing and characterizing new families of continuous probability distributions.

A major outcome of this research was the development of The Sine–Fréchet-G Family of Continuous Distributions: Sub-models, Properties, Simulation and Applications . This work introduced a flexible family of continuous distributions based on the Fréchet generator and investigated its theoretical properties and practical applications.

The research:

  • Derived important statistical properties of the proposed family and its sub-models, including moments, quantile functions, and hazard-rate functions.
  • Estimated the model parameters using maximum likelihood and evaluated the performance of the estimators through Monte Carlo simulations.
  • Demonstrated the practical usefulness of the proposed distributions by fitting them to real-world datasets and comparing their performance with established competing models.

Interactive Application: Sine Fréchet Explorer

To support the practical use and verification of the proposed Sine-Fréchet generalized class, I developed an interactive Shiny web application, deployed for public use. Students and researchers can use the app to replicate the analyses presented in my thesis, run their own simulations, or customize new compound continuous models and apply them to new datasets.

Undergraduate Research Project

Statistical Properties of the Lomax–Inverse Exponential Distribution and Applications to Real-Life Data
I completed my undergraduate research at Modibbo Adama University, Yola, formerly known as Modibbo Adama University of Technology, under the supervision of Professor Sauta S. Abdulkadir.

The project focused on developing and studying a hybrid distribution obtained by integrating the inverse exponential distribution into the Lomax G family. I derived key statistical properties of the proposed distribution and evaluated its flexibility using real-life datasets. The results showed that the distribution provided a better fit than several existing models. This project and some other related laid the foundation for my continued research in probability distribution theory.

Publication:
Abdulkadir, S. S., Jerry, J., & Ieren, T. G. (2020). FUDMA Journal of Sciences, 680–694.
